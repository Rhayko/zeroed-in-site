The business problem

One shift, one trustworthy record.

Dispatch reporting combines North/South site and AM/PM shift records. Inconsistent formatting can split groups; repeat submissions inflate totals; conflicting versions can quietly change the answer. This project establishes a documented acceptance contract before a dashboard receives the data.

The fixture extends the Operations KPI Dashboard’s 528 synthetic shift keys from July–December 2025. Defects are deliberately injected and independently checked against the original baseline. These are demonstration results from a controlled simulation.

01 / Standardize

Change presentation, preserve meaning.

Trim whitespace, map declared categories, parse explicit date formats and validate numeric syntax. Record every before/after change.

02 / Validate

Reconcile the business facts.

Check required fields, unique keys, dates, positive denominators, subset bounds and order/late-reason totals. Keep missing values distinct from zero.

03 / Resolve

Hold uncertainty for review.

Remove repeat submissions with lineage. If two versions disagree, hold both. Never impute missing facts or choose a winner without source evidence.

What the run found

Of the 36 quarantined rows, 24 fail field or business rules and 12 are conflicting versions of six keys. That leaves 30 underlying shift keys requiring review. Twelve repeated rows match exactly; eight match after safe normalization.

There are 68 normalization events across 48 submitted rows, including duplicate submissions. Every input row has exactly one final disposition: 498 + 36 + 12 + 8 = 554.

The accepted subset has a 92.42% on-time rate across 118,000 orders. Excluded records change the population. This is not evidence that cleaning improved operational performance, and it should not replace full-period reporting until exceptions are resolved.

Honest scope

A batch demonstration, not a live ingestion service. It cannot detect every plausible-but-wrong value, authenticate sources or determine which conflicting record is true.
